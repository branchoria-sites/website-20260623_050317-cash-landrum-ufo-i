## Introduction

[Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }})’s hair loss became one of the most visually striking claims associated with the Cash–Landrum incident. The key question, however, is not whether she lost hair but whether the pattern of that loss resembled radiation-induced alopecia. Available descriptions, photographs discussed in contemporary interviews, and later medical commentary point to a dispute that centres on scalp distribution rather than the mere fact of baldness. Critics of the radiation explanation have repeatedly argued that Cash’s reported bald areas looked more like patchy [alopecia areata]({{ 'alopecia-areata/' | relative_url }})—an autoimmune condition that commonly produces discrete round bald spots—than the more diffuse or field-specific hair loss usually associated with substantial radiation exposure. Supporters of the radiation interpretation counter that unusual exposures need not follow textbook patterns. The result is a debate in which scalp pattern is important evidence, but not decisive proof of either explanation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 18, 2005 — He found that Cash&#x27;s doctor&#x27;s notes attribute her hair loss to the autoimmune disease alopecia areata, that her other...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 18, 2005</span></span></span>

Interview notes described photographs showing significant [hair loss]({{ 'hair-loss/' | relative_url }}) affecting the side and crown of Cash’s scalp rather than a uniform thinning across the entire head. Later discussions of the case repeatedly referred to distinct bald areas rather than complete scalp-wide shedding. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 18, 2005 — He found that Cash&#x27;s doctor&#x27;s notes attribute her hair loss to the autoimmune disease alopecia areata, that her other...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 18, 2005</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because clinicians often begin by examining distribution before considering cause. Hair loss confined to recognisable patches raises different diagnostic possibilities from hair loss that appears evenly across a broad exposed area. Contemporary dermatology references describe alopecia areata as typically producing round or oval patches with relatively normal-looking skin between affected areas. The condition can involve one patch or several scattered patches across the scalp. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.scarringalopecia.org/medical-articles/histopathologic-approach-to-alopecia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.scarringalopecia.org">[blog.scarringalopecia.org+2EuCTIN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.scarringalopecia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Histopathologic approach to alopecia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 10, 2020 — 10 Feb 2020 — Alopecia areata AA is characterized by round or oval, smooth patchy hair loss involving any hair-bearin...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2020</span></span></span>

Because Cash’s reported bald areas were described as discrete patches, critics argued that the morphology aligned more naturally with alopecia areata. The case became even more controversial because later summaries of medical records indicated that a scalp biopsy was interpreted as supporting alopecia areata rather than radiation injury. Scalp biopsy is one of the principal tools dermatologists use when the cause of hair loss is uncertain, precisely because different disorders affect follicles in different ways under microscopic examination. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2Healthline]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">January 18, 2005 — He found that Cash&#x27;s doctor&#x27;s notes attribute her hair loss to the autoimmune disease alopecia areata, that her other...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 18, 2005</span></span></span>

A patchy pattern may suggest alopecia areata, but a biopsy can potentially strengthen or weaken that impression. Advocates of the incident’s radiation hypothesis have questioned the interpretation of the pathology findings, arguing that the records are incomplete and that alternative readings are possible. ## Why Pattern Alone Cannot Settle Causation

Although scalp distribution is a significant clue, it cannot by itself determine what caused Cash’s hair loss.

First, hair disorders do not always present in textbook form. Even well-known conditions can produce atypical appearances.
