## Introduction

The Cash-Landrum UFO incident was a reported close encounter on 29 December 1980 near Dayton, Texas, in which [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}), [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}), and Landrum’s young grandson Colby said they saw a huge, heat-emitting, diamond-shaped object above a rural road, followed by a group of military-style helicopters. The case matters because it moved beyond a typical sighting story: the witnesses alleged physical injury, pursued government accountability, and eventually became involved in a $20 million federal lawsuit that was dismissed in 1986.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TRANSCRIPT OF BERGSTROM AFB INTERVIEW OF BETTY CASH, VICKIE &amp; COLBY LANDRUM August 1981. Part 1 of 2. TRANSCRIPT. of taped interview...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1981</span></span></span>

The incident remains one of the most debated American UFO cases because its strongest features are also its weak points. The witnesses gave detailed, emotionally forceful accounts and reported serious symptoms, especially Betty Cash. Yet investigators never established what [the object]({{ 'the-object/' | relative_url }}) was, whether the helicopters belonged to the US military, whether the injuries were caused by the encounter, or even the precise [location]({{ 'location/' | relative_url }}) where the most intense part of the event allegedly occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org+2badufos.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TRANSCRIPT OF BERGSTROM AFB INTERVIEW OF BETTY CASH, VICKIE &amp; COLBY LANDRUM August 1981. ## What the witnesses said happened that night

Cash, Landrum, and Colby Landrum said they were driving home towards Dayton after an evening meal when they noticed a bright light above the trees. At first, according to later summaries of their account, they thought it might be an [aircraft]({{ 'aircraft/' | relative_url }}) approaching Houston Intercontinental Airport, which was not far away by regional standards. As the road wound through the wooded area, they said the light seemed much closer and brighter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

The object was later described as large, upright, and diamond-shaped, with intense light, heat, and flames or fire coming from its lower section. The witnesses said the heat was so strong that the car’s metal surfaces became painful to touch; Vickie Landrum also claimed that a handprint was left in the softened dashboard vinyl, though later discussions of the case note the absence of strong photographic or physical documentation for some of these trace claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

The most unusual part of the account came after the object reportedly rose above the trees. Cash and Landrum said numerous helicopters appeared and seemed to surround or accompany it. Later descriptions often identify some of them as tandem-rotor CH-47 Chinook-type helicopters, a detail that became central to the claim that the incident involved a government or military operation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

A useful caution is that the description sharpened over time. A later Bergstrom Air Force Base interview records the witnesses discussing the event with Air Force officers on 17 August 1981, but sceptical and archival discussions note that earlier reports were less certain about the exact shape, with the “diamond” description becoming more prominent as the case developed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TRANSCRIPT OF BERGSTROM AFB INTERVIEW OF BETTY CASH, VICKIE &amp; COLBY LANDRUM August 1981. ## Why the injuries made the case so hard to dismiss

The Cash-Landrum case became famous largely because the witnesses did not merely report seeing something strange. They said they became ill afterwards. The reported symptoms included nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, weakness, eye irritation, sunburn-like skin effects, blisters, and hair loss, with Betty Cash said to have suffered the most severe problems and to have required hospital treatment in early 1981.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

For UFO researchers, those symptoms made the case unusually compelling: here was an alleged sighting with claimed medical consequences, not just lights in the sky. The Blue Blurry Lines document collection shows how much of the later case file revolved around [medical summaries]({{ 'medical-gaps/' | relative_url }}), MUFON records, correspondence, hypnosis files, legal material, and interviews, including a Peter Rank medical-record analysis listed as a “complete summary report” from April 1981.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.blueblurrylines.com/2013/07/resource-guide-for-cash-landrum-ufo-case.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blueblurrylines.com">[blueblurrylines.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blueblurrylines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blue Blurry Lines: The Cash-Landrum UFO Case Document Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue Blurry Lines: The Cash-Landrum UFO Case Document Collection</span></span></span>

The medical interpretation, however, has never been straightforward. Some symptoms were said to resemble radiation exposure, but sceptical commentators and later case reviewers have argued that the timing and survival of the witnesses do not fit a simple high-dose ionising-radiation scenario. Enigma Labs’ case summary, for example, notes both the claims of physical illness and the sceptical counterpoint that Cash’s doctor’s notes have been interpreted as pointing to alopecia rather than radiation-induced hair loss.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

That distinction matters. “Radiation burns” became the memorable public shorthand, but shorthand is not the same as diagnosis. A heat source, ultraviolet exposure, chemical exposure, pre-existing illness, stress-related symptoms, or unrelated medical conditions have all been discussed in attempts to make sense of the symptoms. None has been proved as the cause of the witnesses’ condition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

## The helicopter question became the hinge of the whole case

The witnesses’ legal and public argument depended heavily on the helicopters. If the helicopters were military, then the US government might plausibly have known what was happening or been involved. If the helicopters could not be linked to any government operation, the legal claim was much harder to sustain, even if the witnesses were sincere and even if they had genuinely been injured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

There was at least one important corroborating claim. A Dayton police officer, Lamar Walker, and his wife Marie were later reported by UFO investigators to have seen roughly a dozen Chinook-type helicopters in the area that night, though they did not report seeing the UFO itself. That kind of secondary helicopter testimony kept the case alive because it suggested that Cash, Landrum, and Colby were not alone in seeing unusual air traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

The formal problem was that investigators did not find a military paper trail. In 1982, Lt Col George Sarran of the Department of the Army Inspector General investigated the allegation and found no evidence that the helicopters reported by the witnesses belonged to the US Armed Forces. A later summary of the case says Sarran considered the witnesses credible, but credibility was not the same as proof of government responsibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

That gap explains why the case sits uneasily between belief and dismissal. The helicopter claims are specific enough to be investigated, but the search for unit records, missions, or official acknowledgement did not produce the kind of documentation that would anchor the story in a confirmed military event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

## Why the lawsuit failed

Cash and Landrum contacted public officials, including US senators, and were eventually directed towards the [claims process]({{ 'claims-process/' | relative_url }}) at Bergstrom Air Force Base. The witnesses later sued the US federal government for $20 million, arguing that they had been harmed by a government-linked incident. The dismissal did not require the court to prove what the object was. ## The strongest reasons people still take it seriously

The case has endured because several elements are difficult to wave away casually. The witnesses repeatedly described a frightening event, they pursued official channels rather than only media attention, and the case generated interviews, medical discussion, legal filings, and years of investigation. The Bergstrom interview transcript shows that the story entered formal claims channels within the military bureaucracy, not only UFO magazines or television reconstructions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TRANSCRIPT OF BERGSTROM AFB INTERVIEW OF BETTY CASH, VICKIE &amp; COLBY LANDRUM August 1981. ## The strongest reasons for caution

The central weakness is the lack of hard physical evidence. Claims about scorched road surfaces, heated pavement, softened dashboard material, and radiation effects are powerful, but later sceptical reviews stress that clear photographs, uncontested samples, or official measurements proving such traces are missing or inadequate. Robert Sheaffer’s sceptical review highlights the absence of photographs or physical evidence for alleged [road marks]({{ 'road-marks/' | relative_url }}) and points to uncertainty about the exact sighting location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://badufos.blogspot.com/2013/11/between-beer-joint-and-some-kind-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: badufos.blogspot.com">[badufos.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">badufos.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unravels...</span></span></span>

The location problem is especially damaging. If investigators could not precisely identify where the object hovered, then claims about scorched road, damaged trees, soil samples, or residual radiation become far harder to verify. Sheaffer cites a Texas Department of Health-related account in which investigator Charles Russ Meyer recorded that Schuessler said the witnesses could only place the sighting broadly on a stretch of road between a beer joint and a highway warning sign.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://badufos.blogspot.com/2013/11/between-beer-joint-and-some-kind-of.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: badufos.blogspot.com">[badufos.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">badufos.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unravels...</span></span></span>

There are also inconsistencies and developments in the witness narrative. Later accounts emphasised a diamond-shaped craft, flames, blue lights, and numerous helicopters, but earlier documented comments were reportedly less definite about the object’s shape. In any witness case, especially one involving fear, bright light, heat, night driving, later interviews, media attention, and legal pressure, memory development is a real evidential issue.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

The medical evidence is contested as well. ## What can and cannot be concluded

The most careful conclusion is that the Cash-Landrum incident is a significant unresolved UFO case, not a proved alien encounter and not a proved secret US military operation. The witnesses’ account is detailed, the alleged injuries were serious, and the helicopter claims gave investigators a concrete lead. Yet the available public evidence has not established the nature of the object, the ownership of the helicopters, or a firm causal link between the reported encounter and the medical symptoms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia+2blueblurrylines.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

The case is best understood as a collision between three kinds of evidence: human testimony, medical claims, and missing institutional records. Believers tend to emphasise the sincerity of the witnesses, the severity of the symptoms, and the apparent helicopter corroboration. Sceptics tend to emphasise the lack of physical traces, uncertain location, disputed medical interpretation, and failure to identify any military unit or operation. Both sides are responding to real features of the record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/cashlani.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[cufon.org+2badufos.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bergstrom AFB Interview of Betty Cash, Vickie</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TRANSCRIPT OF BERGSTROM AFB INTERVIEW OF BETTY CASH, VICKIE &amp; COLBY LANDRUM August 1981. Part 1 of 2. TRANSCRIPT. of taped interview...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1981</span></span></span>

What would change the assessment would be unusually concrete evidence: authenticated military flight records matching the date and area, reliable medical documentation clearly tying the symptoms to a specific exposure, contemporaneous photographs or samples from the scene, or independent witness reports made immediately after the event. In the absence of that, the Cash-Landrum case remains compelling, troubling, and unresolved, but not demonstrably solved.

    return encodeURIComponent(String(value || '').replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim()).replace(/%20/g, '+');
  }
  function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket];
    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
