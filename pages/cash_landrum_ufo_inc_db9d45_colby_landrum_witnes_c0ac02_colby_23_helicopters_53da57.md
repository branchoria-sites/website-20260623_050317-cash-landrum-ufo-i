## Introduction

The claim that **23 military helicopters accompanied [the object]({{ 'the-object/' | relative_url }}) in the Cash–Landrum incident** is one of the most frequently repeated details in the entire case. It is also one of the least straightforward. Although the number often appears in retellings as if it were a carefully verified count, the figure emerged from witness observations made at night, during a highly stressful event, and partly from the perspective of seven-year-old [Colby]({{ 'colby/' | relative_url }}) Landrum, who was frightened and watching from inside a moving vehicle. The question is not whether helicopters were reported at all, but whether the precise number “23” deserves the confidence later writers often gave it. The history of the claim shows how an approximate observation can gradually harden into an apparently exact fact. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

## How the Count Entered the Story

The helicopter element became central because it appeared to connect the reported object to the US military. According to the witnesses, a group of helicopters arrived around the glowing diamond-shaped craft after it rose above the trees. Later accounts commonly stated that [Betty Cash]({{ 'betty-cash/' | relative_url }}) and [Vickie Landrum]({{ 'vickie-landrum/' | relative_url }}) counted **23 helicopters**, many of them identified as CH-47 Chinooks. The figure became important in both UFO literature and the later lawsuit against the federal government because a formation that large would be difficult to dismiss as a simple misidentification. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

What is often lost in later retellings is that the number originated from witness observation during the event itself rather than from any independent record. No flight logs, military documents, radar records, photographs, or other contemporaneous evidence confirmed that exactly 23 helicopters were present. The number survived because the witnesses repeated it, not because it was externally verified. Court proceedings and government investigations ultimately failed to establish military ownership of the reported helicopters. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

For a page focused on Colby Landrum's perspective, this distinction matters. The famous helicopter count is not a measured quantity. It is a remembered quantity.

## Counting Under Stress at Night

The reliability problem is not that Colby was a child. Modern eyewitness research generally finds that children can accurately remember the central features of emotionally significant events. The difficulty lies in the specific task involved here: counting many similar objects under poor viewing conditions.

Several factors worked against precise counting:

* The observation reportedly occurred at night.
* The witnesses were dealing with an intensely bright object that drew attention away from the helicopters.
* The helicopters were moving rather than stationary.
* The witnesses were inside a vehicle for much of the encounter.
* Fear and confusion were already present, especially for Colby, who later developed a pronounced fear of helicopters associated with the event. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://podcasts.happyscribe.com/so-supernatural/alien-the-cash-landrum-incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: podcasts.happyscribe.com">[Podcasts - Your Podcast Transcripts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">podcasts.happyscribe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vicky&#x27;s grandson, Colby, is now seven, and he&#x27;s developed a fear of helicopters ever since what happened.Read more...</span></span></span>

Even under ordinary circumstances, distinguishing between 18, 23, or 28 [aircraft]({{ 'aircraft/' | relative_url }}) in a shifting formation would be difficult. Under stressful conditions, people often remember the presence of “many” objects more reliably than the exact number. The difference between recognising a swarm of helicopters and accurately counting every aircraft is substantial.

This issue becomes sharper when viewed through the lens of a frightened child. Colby's value as a witness is strongest when confirming broad features of the event—something unusual, helicopters, fear, and subsequent discussion. It is weaker when used to support an exact numerical claim.

## Why Precision Became More Certain Later

This reflects a common pattern in high-profile cases. Early uncertainty tends to disappear as a story is repeated. Once a specific number enters the narrative, later authors often reproduce it without re-examining how the number was obtained. Over time, readers encounter “23 helicopters” so often that the figure acquires an authority independent of its original source.

The Cash–Landrum case offers an example of this process. The statement evolved from a witness count into a defining feature of the legend. Yet the underlying evidential basis remained the same: human observation during a brief, stressful, nighttime event. No new independent confirmation transformed the estimate into a verified count. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

## Can the 23 Helicopters Be Trusted?

The most defensible conclusion is neither that [the count]({{ 'the-count/' | relative_url }}) was correct nor that it was invented. Instead, the evidence supports a more limited claim.

The witnesses consistently reported **a large number of helicopters** associated with the event. That general observation is deeply embedded in the case record and was repeated for years. What is much harder to establish is that the witnesses, including a frightened seven-year-old observer, accurately determined that the number was exactly 23. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash%E2%80%93Landrum_incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cash–Landrum incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cash–Landrum incident</span></span></span>

For historians, investigators, and sceptical analysts, the helicopter count therefore functions best as a reminder of the difference between a core observation and a precise measurement. The core observation is that helicopters were reportedly present in significant numbers.
